Before you trust a collision probability, ask four questions.

A Conjunction Data Message is one forecast of one close approach, reissued every few hours. It reports Pc, but not always the assumptions behind it or whether they hold for this encounter. Run these checks on every message, and decide on the history, not the last number.

Four questions on every CDM

Ask them in this order, at every decision point. Each one checks an assumption the reported Pc relies on.

  1. 1

    Miss and PcHow close? How likely?

    How to check

    Rebuild Pc yourself from your own independent data: combine both covariances in the same reference frame (primary RTN at the TCA), project them onto the encounter plane and integrate over the hard-body disc. Compare your value with the reported one.

    Warning sign

    A reported Pc sitting at a floor value, such as 10−30. The floor is not reassurance: it says the number is very small, not why. A gap between your Pc and the reported one means the assumptions differ.

  2. 2

    EnvelopeHow big is the uncertainty, against the miss?

    How to check

    Form the combined relative covariance, with the secondary’s covariance rotated into the primary’s RTN frame. Compare its longest 1σ axis with the miss distance. Check the environment and physical models, and the number of measurements of both objects. Check if the envelope is correlated with the calculation assumptions.

    Warning sign

    An envelope that shrinks faster than the miss. A low Pc can come from a small envelope rather than a large miss. Size any manoeuvre on Pc max, the worst case over envelope size.

  3. 3

    ConsistencyDid the new forecast move beyond its own envelope?

    How to check

    Measure each revision against the previous message’s envelope. Check the stability of the direction and magnitude of the principal components of the covariance over time.

    Warning sign

    An unstable and inconsistent direction and magnitude of the covariance means a low confidence level.

  4. 4

    Encounter planeIs the miss along the envelope, or across it?

    How to check

    In the encounter plane perpendicular to the relative velocity, find α, the angle between the miss and the envelope’s long axis, and the miss in sigmas: d = √((mmajor/smajor)2 + (mminor/sminor)2). Drag the bar below to demonstrate.

    Warning sign

    Along the long axis, with d near 1: a real risk, even below policy. Across a thin axis, with a large d: a tiny Pc that is only as safe as that thin axis is right.

Turn the four questions into a routine

Check the assumptions behind Pc.

Check the history, not just the last number.

Decide while the options remain.
